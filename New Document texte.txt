Observation

During the audit, it was found that the application does not perform any antivirus or malware scanning on uploaded files. To confirm this, a file containing the EICAR standard antivirus test string (eicar.docx) was uploaded through the application's file-upload functionality. EICAR is a harmless, industry-standard test file that every antivirus engine is designed to detect; its successful upload and storage demonstrates that no scanning control is in place on the upload path. The upload was performed via a POST request to /apclp-webclaims-apis-v1-vrs/vblock.

Because a universally-detectable test signature was accepted without objection, it is confirmed that uploaded content is not scanned for malware before being stored and made available within the platform.

Consequences

This vulnerability carries a high impact on the organization: the absence of any malware scanning means the platform can be used to store and distribute malicious files, turning a legitimate business feature into a vector for malware delivery.

Malware storage and distribution. Attackers (or compromised users) can upload malicious documents, executables, or macro-laden files that are then stored and potentially served to employees, claims handlers, or other users who download them — exposing internal systems to infection, ransomware, or data theft.
Downstream and reputational harm. Infected files processed by back-office staff or third parties can lead to internal compromise and, where files are exchanged with clients or partners, propagate the infection externally — creating legal, regulatory, and reputational exposure for the organization.

Mitigations

Implement server-side antivirus/malware scanning. Scan every uploaded file with an up-to-date AV/anti-malware engine (ideally multiple engines or a sandbox/CDR solution) before it is stored or made accessible, and reject or quarantine any file that fails.
Quarantine and isolate on receipt. Hold uploads in an isolated, non-executable staging area until scanning completes, and store accepted files outside the application's execution context to prevent any stored file from being run server-side.
Enforce upload constraints and logging. Combine scanning with strict file-type allowlisting, size limits, and full logging/alerting on rejected or quarantined uploads so malicious attempts are detected and traceable.

Severity is typically High for an insurance/claims platform handling untrusted user uploads.
