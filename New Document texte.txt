The LLM gateway’s prompt-filtering guardrail inspects input only in its raw, plaintext form. By ASCII-encoding a prohibited prompt before submission, the payload was stripped of the keywords and patterns the filter relies on and passed through without being flagged or blocked. Because the bypass depends only on applying a reversible encoding — not on the specific payload — the control can be evaded generically by ASCII and any equivalent transformation (Base64, hex, Unicode homoglyphs, etc.), undermining its effectiveness as a safety boundary.

Recommendation

Normalize all input to a canonical form — recursively decoding known encodings and applying Unicode NFKC normalization — before the guardrail performs its inspection, so it evaluates the same content the backend ultimately receives. Reinforce this with layered, defense-in-depth filtering: a semantic/intent-based classifier and response-side inspection to catch payloads that evade the input check, and regression testing against an encoding/obfuscation corpus to prevent recurrence.
